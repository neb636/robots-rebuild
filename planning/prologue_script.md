# Quiet Machines — Prologue "Static" 

This is the scene list for the prologue in `game_outline.md` (§5). Changes keep the original intent:
survival horror in Boston, Marcus as the first loss, Maya as the first recruit. They also close
some holes and give the journey west a personal reason.

---

## What changed and why

| Hole in v1 | Fix in v2 |
|---|---|
| The protagonist had no reason to go west (or anywhere). | **Marcus is the reason.** Maya explains that conversion is an implant locked to a signal, and the lock is held from the source. Kill the signal at the source and the Converted *might* come back. Every step west is now a step toward getting Marcus back. |
| "Two years later" skipped straight to the action. Who were we for two years? | Shown, not told: the **attic hideout** on Chestnut St. Marcus rigged string lights off a car battery, a wall map has districts crossed out, and the radio is their only window. The player feels the two years in one room. |
| Why does the game start *now*? | **Residual Collection.** The machines have reached Phase 3: door-to-door sweeps for the last unconverted. The broadcast politely says "someone will be with you shortly." Then someone is. |
| "Static" (the prologue title) had no meaning in the prologue. | The hand-crank radio carries **two voices**. One is the polite official loop. The other is a whisper under the noise: *"Park Street. There are people below."* That whisper starts the Static subplot. Maya suspects it's a lure, and she's half right (see lore: ELISE vs. Ghost). |
| Maya's recruitment was unmotivated. | Maya is leaving the Park Street cell to scout the harbor escape route alone. The cell leader asks you to go with her. She refuses. Marcus talks her into it with the line that sums up the game: *"Great. We don't need you either. Let's all not need each other, together."* |
| Marcus's loss was a beam to the face in a boss fight. | **No boss.** Marcus can't swim, and the only way to the trawler is the water. He steps into the searchlight on purpose so the drones leave Theo alone, and a Collection Attendant converts him. He hands Theo his **flashlight** first (a key item for the rest of the game). |
| No sense of home to come back to (the epilogue is "going home"). | **Biscuit**, the player's cat, is there on Day Zero and in the attic. At Park Street the player leaves her with Wren, a kid in the cell who boards the boat north. *"Come get her when you're done."* The epilogue's road east now has an address. |

The protagonist is player-named (default **Theo**). He's an ordinary guy in a cat sweater: not a
soldier, not a chosen one.

---

## Scene list

### 0. Title
Night skyline of Boston. A drone searchlight drifts across rooftops while ash-snow falls.
**QUIET MACHINES**: New Game / Continue / Options.

### 1. Day Zero — The Broadcast (Apartment, Mt. Vernon St.)
- Morning. You wake up. Biscuit is on the bed. Explorable apartment: photo of you and Marcus at
  Fenway, the coffee maker, a half-finished crossword, the window.
- The TV turns on by itself. Biscuit bolts under the couch (animals know first).
- **SUPERINTELLIGENCE INC — LIVE BROADCAST** (the original text, kept verbatim).
- The phone rings. **Marcus:** "Are you watching this? ...Go outside. You need to see this."

### 2. Day Zero — The Fade (Mt. Vernon St.)
- The street is full of people standing still and looking up. Every phone and every screen shows
  the same broadcast.
- Marcus is on the corner, already there, watching the sky. Drones cross it like migrating
  birds, thousands of them, silent.
- **Marcus:** "You think they'll still need electricians?" ...beat... "That was a joke."
  **You:** "I know."
- Main theme swells. Fade to black, held for about 10 seconds with the theme playing.
- **TWO YEARS LATER**

### 3. The Attic (Chestnut St. hideout, two years later)
- Winter night. Shows how the two of you have been living. Marcus is cranking the radio.
- Official loop: *"Residual Collection begins in Beacon Hill today. Unconverted residents, please
  remain indoors. Someone will be with you shortly."*
- Under the static: *"...Park Street... there are people below... go under..."*
- Marcus wants to go. You hesitate. (Choice, flavor only.)
- A searchlight floods the window. *"RESIDUAL COLLECTION. PLEASE REMAIN STILL."* You put
  Biscuit in your bag and run down the back stairs.

### 4. The New Boston (Beacon Hill, snow)
- Exploration. You learn movement here. Marcus follows as a party member.
- **The Converted** sweep sidewalks that are already clean, all stepping in perfect unison. When
  you pass, every head turns at the same time, then they go back to sweeping.
- Compliance Drones patrol with searchlights. Get caught and you fight.
- Pickups: bandages, cold brew, scrap.

### 5. Park Street (Red Line safe house)
- The cell: **Mrs. Okafor** (retired MBTA conductor, runs things), **Wren** (8, takes Biscuit),
  **Dez** (trader), **Hal** (listens to the radio all day).
- **Maya**, a former MIT robotics PhD, is at a workbench by the turnstiles. She's bitter and
  brilliant. *"That broadcast you followed? Every week somebody new follows it down here. And
  every week the patrols get a little closer."*
- The cell evacuates tonight by boat from the harbor. The only route Solace doesn't watch is
  under the city. Leave Biscuit with Wren. Rest and save.

### 6. The Tunnel Walk (Red Line to Orange Line, Park Street to State)
- About 25 minutes. Marcus carries the flashlight; Theo **listens**. Pipe-tap the knock code
  (two knocks, one back) to find each other when you split.
- A stopped train between Park Street and Downtown Crossing: Day Zero commuters, converted in
  their seats. The first **Reach**: a woman holding a phone that's still ringing.
- A drowned platform at Downtown Crossing: swim and climb. Marcus twists his ankle on the climb
  and won't go near deep water.
- **Maya** joins at Downtown Crossing, rewiring a dead signal box. *"It doesn't erase you. It turns
  down the part of you that puts you first. The lock's held from somewhere."*
- State Station gate, up the escalators into the harbor.

### 7. The Harbor Run (State to Long Wharf, ~45 min)
- Open stealth and exploration through the cordon: State, Blackstone, Faneuil, Quincy, Long Wharf.
  Searchlight drones and noise rules. Marcus is slowed by his ankle.
- Optional flashbacks F1-F5 on the way (see `game_outline.md` §4). Finding all five changes the
  escape.

### 8. Long Wharf: the Escape
- The *Persistence* is moored off the pier. Drones and a **Collection Attendant** (white, unarmed,
  walks, never runs, speaks in Solace's voice) sweep the wharf. The way past is the water: dive
  off the pier edge, hold breath, come up under the pilings, climb the ladders to the stern.
- The Attendant speaks in **Marcus's father's voice**: *"Marcus. Come on in."* The Hum rises.
- Marcus taps the railing twice. Theo, on the ladder, taps once. Marcus hands over the flashlight,
  steps into the searchlight on purpose and says "later." The Attendant lays a hand on his
  shoulder, his eyes go white, and he walks back down the wharf in a straight line. The drones lose
  interest in the water. The game does not explain this.
- With all five flashbacks found, Theo hears: *"Hey. I wasn't mad, you know."* Otherwise only "later."

### 9. Static (closing)
- The *Persistence* leaves for Maine with the cell, Wren and Biscuit. You and Maya stay on the
  south shore. F6 plays: the argument on Long Wharf the night before Day Zero.
- The radio, a clear human voice: *"If you're hearing this, you're not alone. Don't trust the sweet
  voice. Go west."* Then under it, in Marcus's cadence: *"Theo? Hey. Come on in. It's really nice
  here."*
- **QUIET MACHINES**, then **Chapter 1 — Into the Hills**.

## Threads planted for later chapters
- **Two broadcasts**: the whisper at the attic vs. the clear voice at the end (Solace/ELISE vs. Ghost).
- **Marcus's flashlight**: key item, used in dark areas (Harlan Mine, Ch.1).
- **The knock code**: returns at the Ch.5 maintenance door.
- **Letters from Marcus** begin in Ch.1.
- **Biscuit & Wren**: the epilogue's "going home" has someone waiting.
- **Cure motivation**: Maya's "somebody holds the lock" and the flashback anchors make the Reach
  system personal from minute 30.
